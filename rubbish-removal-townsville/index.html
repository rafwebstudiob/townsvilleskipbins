Rubbish Removal Townsville: Skip Bins vs Pick-up

In short: for a big clean-up, a skip bin is often the easier way to remove household or green waste in Townsville. For small amounts, council's free yearly hard waste collection and its waste facilities are worth knowing about.

The main ways to get rid of household and garden rubbish in Townsville, what each one takes, and when a skip bin makes more sense.

Last updated: 27 October 2026

Your options for rubbish removal in Townsville

In short: For a big clean-up, a skip bin is usually the simplest way to remove household or garden waste. For a small amount, council's yearly kerbside hard waste collection or a trip to the tip may be enough.

Most Townsville households have three ways to get rid of more rubbish than fits in their bins:

  1. A skip bin, delivered to your driveway and taken away when you're done.
  2. Taking it to the tip yourself, at Townsville City Council's Stuart Waste Facility or the Hervey Range Resource Recovery Centre.
  3. Council's free kerbside hard waste collection, booked once each financial year, or two tip vouchers instead.

Which one makes sense depends on how much you have, what it is, and whether you have a trailer and the time to do the runs.

Option 1: a skip bin

In short: A skip suits jobs with a lot of mixed rubbish, heavy waste or many trailer loads of garden waste, because you load it at your own pace and it's taken away in one go.

A skip sits on your driveway (or on the street with council approval) for an agreed hire period. You can fill it over a few days, and you don't need a trailer, a ute or anyone to lift heavy items. Skips can take things council's hard waste collection won't, such as green waste, bricks, concrete, soil and builders' waste, as long as you choose the right bin and tell the operator. They can't take asbestos, gas bottles, batteries, tyres or chemicals in a normal load. See what can go in a skip and the size guide.

For a deceased estate, a house move or a big shed or garage job, see our clean-outs guide.

Option 2: the Stuart Waste Facility or Hervey Range

In short: If you have a trailer and only a load or two, you can take rubbish to council's waste facilities yourself. Council says domestic green waste and recyclables are free to drop off.

Townsville City Council's Stuart Waste Facility is at 24 Vantassel Street, Stuart, and council lists it as open daily from 6:30am to 5:45pm. The Hervey Range Resource Recovery Centre at 811 Hervey Range Road, Alice River, is open daily from 7am to 4:45pm and takes small loads only. Both are closed on Good Friday and Christmas Day. Council says disposal of domestic green waste and recyclables is free, so sorting your load before you go can help.

Option 3: council's kerbside hard waste collection

In short: Each household can book one free kerbside hard waste collection each financial year, of up to 2 cubic metres. From 3 August 2026, you can choose two tip vouchers instead.

Townsville City Council offers residents one free booked kerbside hard waste collection each financial year, of up to 2 m³. Council says that from 3 August 2026, residents can choose two tip vouchers instead of the collection. Items should be out by 6am on your collection day, and no more than two days before.

  • It takes: household furniture, whitegoods (with doors removed), e-waste, bikes and scrap metal.
  • It won't take: bricks, concrete, tyres, car parts, builders' waste, gas bottles, glass, hazardous waste, liquids or green waste.

What about my kerbside bins?

In short: Your bins are for everyday rubbish and recycling, not clean-ups.

Townsville City Council collects the red-lid general waste bin weekly and the yellow-lid recycling bin fortnightly. Most properties don't have a council green waste bin, so larger amounts of garden waste can go to a council waste facility, where domestic green waste is free.

So which is easier?

In short: Pick a skip for big, mixed or heavy jobs. Use the hard waste collection or the tip for a small amount.

  • A skip bin usually makes sense for a full clean-out, a renovation, a big garden or shed job, heavy waste, or when you don't have a trailer or the time for several tip runs.
  • Council's hard waste collection suits up to 2 m³ of furniture, whitegoods or scrap metal once a year.
  • The Stuart Waste Facility or Hervey Range suits one or two trailer loads, especially green waste and recycling.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest way to get rid of a lot of rubbish in Townsville?

It depends on how much you have and what it is. For one or two loads, a trailer run to the Stuart Waste Facility or the Hervey Range Resource Recovery Centre may be enough. For a big clean-out, heavy waste or many loads, a skip bin is often the easier option, because you load it at your own pace and it's taken away in one trip.

Where is the Townsville tip and when is it open?

Townsville City Council's Stuart Waste Facility is at 24 Vantassel Street, Stuart, open daily from 6:30am to 5:45pm. The Hervey Range Resource Recovery Centre at 811 Hervey Range Road, Alice River, is open daily from 7am to 4:45pm for small loads. Both are closed on Good Friday and Christmas Day.

Does Townsville City Council pick up bulky rubbish?

Yes. Each household can book one free kerbside hard waste collection each financial year, of up to 2 cubic metres. Council says that from 3 August 2026 you can choose two tip vouchers instead.

What won't Townsville's hard waste collection take?

Council lists bricks, concrete, tyres, car parts, builders' waste, gas bottles, glass, hazardous waste, liquids and green waste as not accepted. A skip bin is one way to deal with green waste and building rubbish.

Is green waste free at the Townsville tip?

Council says disposal of domestic green waste and recyclables is free at its waste facilities.

Can a skip bin take garden waste and building rubbish?

Usually, yes, which is one reason people choose a skip. Heavy waste such as soil, concrete and bricks normally needs a smaller bin with a weight limit, and asbestos can never go in a general skip. Tell the operator what you have when you request a quote.

Do I need a permit for a skip bin on the street in Townsville?

A skip wholly on your own property generally doesn't need council approval. A skip on the road, footpath or verge usually needs approval from Townsville City Council. See our council skip permits guide for details.
